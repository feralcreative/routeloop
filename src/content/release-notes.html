What's new

Routeloop is in a closed alpha and changes most days. The newest build is at the top, and the version in the page footer tells you which one you are on.

Everything below is headed by day rather than by build. It predates the version stamp — and the alpha — and was read back out of the commit log, so you can see how the app got to you.

31 August 2026 — the map tells you where you run out of fuel

  • Every day now shows how far in each stop is and how far you are on the current tank, counted from the last stop you tagged Gas or Charge. A fuel stop reads zero at its own row, because standing at the pump you have gone no distance on that tank yet.
  • The map marks every place you would run dry, not just the first — a red disc with an E on it, one for each tankful the day needs. Hover one to confirm what it is.
  • The stretch you cannot ride on the fuel you have is drawn as red-and-white hazard tape, from the first empty marker to the next fuel stop, or to the end of the day if there is no fuel stop after it.
  • A dotted red circle around you shrinks to nothing exactly as you reach the point you run dry, and springs back out when you pass a fuel stop. The range is the smallest tank in the group, so the plan is built around whoever has to stop first.
  • All of it is behind one Range button beside the slider, on both the builder and a shared ride. Turn it off and you get the plain route back.
  • The scrubber no longer wastes most of its travel on the overnights. On a nine-day ride, roughly two thirds of the slider used to be nights in hotels with nothing on the map.
  • Searching for a place now looks at the part of the map you can see, and the category chips (Gas, Food, Coffee, Lodging) follow the map rather than the last stop of the day. If nothing matches on screen it says so, and zooming out searches wider.
  • A new Along the day option searches the whole day's route instead of just what is on screen, and lists what it finds nearest-detour-first.
  • Tag a stop in the middle of a day as lodging and the day offers to end there, splitting what follows into the next day. You can also split a day at any stop.
  • Red is no longer offered as a day colour, because red now means "you cannot ride this". Rides you have already coloured keep what they were given.

30 August 2026 — the timeline scrubs one day at a time

  • The slider along the bottom of the builder now covers the day you are working on, not the whole ride. On a four-day trip that is roughly eighteen times the precision — enough to actually find the middle of Saturday afternoon and work out where lunch goes. Whole ride beside the readout still gives you the old full-length slider.
  • The day you are editing is the bright one on the map. It used to be whichever day the slider happened to be parked on, so clicking into day three to work on it left day three faded out behind day one.
  • Picking a category for a waypoint closes the picker. It stayed open and had to be dismissed by hand, which read as the click not having registered.
  • The round feedback badge no longer sits on the end of the slider. It was covering the last of the travel, which is the end of the ride.

30 August 2026 — plan a ride together

  • A ride somebody shared with you now has a way in. If you are on a ride's roster you will see a link to open it from the ride page, and what it says is what you can actually do there — edit it, suggest changes, or leave a comment.
  • Two of you can plan the same ride at the same time. Take different days and neither of you can lose the other's work: your saves are merged a day at a time rather than one of you overwriting the whole ride.
  • You can see who else is in a ride with you and which day they are on. A day somebody else is working on is marked, so you know to expect a clash before you start moving stops around.
  • When someone else saves a day you are looking at, it updates in front of you rather than going quietly out of date. If you have unsaved work that clashes, the panel says so and stops saving instead of picking a winner.
  • Worth re-checking: if you have ever had a ride open in two tabs, or planned one alongside somebody else, look over its days. Until today the last save won outright and could take the other one's changes with it, silently.

29 August 2026 — two fixes and a tidier way around

  • Adding a bike in the Paddock no longer throws you out of every field. Each time you clicked into one it lost focus straight away and had to be clicked again; it just works now. Saving a place or renaming a group had the same problem, and does not.
  • You can set a stop shorter than an hour. Typing .25 gives you a quarter of an hour, and stop times are shown to two decimals so a fifteen-minute coffee stop stays fifteen minutes. It did not before: anything that was not a multiple of six minutes was quietly rounded on its way out of the box, and grew a little every time you touched the row. Worth re-checking any stop time you set before today — a stop you meant as twenty minutes may be sitting at eighteen.
  • Dash is now the first thing in the menu instead of hiding at the top of the Rides list. Your numbers, your records, then your rides.
  • There is a Plan a ride sign at the top of your dashboard. Starting a ride is what this is for and it used to mean opening a menu first.
  • Riders and Friends are one page with two tabs, reached from Riders. Everyone in one place instead of two, and old links to your friends list still land in the right place.
  • You can download a ride straight from the builder now — GPX, KML, GeoJSON, CSV, or a file per day — without going to the Import / Export page.
  • If you imported a route file and then re-cut it here, downloading it gives you your version. It used to hand back the file you uploaded, so an afternoon of edits came back as though you had never made them. What you uploaded is still kept, and is still in your account archive.
  • Google's zoom and map-type controls are about a third smaller, so there is more map to look at.
  • A fix worth knowing about even though it was never reported: if the builder offered to restore unsaved changes, clicking the map to add a point silently did nothing, and the route could not be dragged into shape. It looked completely normal. That is fixed.

28 August 2026 — riding on someone else's ride

  • Everyone you add to a ride can now look at it, talk about it, and suggest changes to it. That is what an invitation gives them from the moment you add them — you no longer have to choose between showing somebody a route and letting them help plan it.
  • Letting somebody edit the ride outright is a separate decision you make yourself, either while you are adding them or any time afterwards, from the Riders page. You can go the other way too and give somebody a look and nothing more.
  • Editing means the route. Deleting the ride, changing who can see it, and adding or removing riders stay with the owner however much you trust somebody.
  • A ride can now have more than one owner, so you are not the single point of failure on a trip you organized. The last owner cannot leave — somebody has to be able to run it.
  • Comments, on the whole ride or on one particular stop. “Is this hotel actually walkable?” belongs on the hotel. Delete the stop later and the comment does not vanish with it — it moves up to the ride and still says which stop it was about.
  • Suggestions. Open a ride you cannot edit, rearrange a day the way you think it should go, and press Suggest. The owner sees it and takes it or leaves it. If they change that day in the meantime, your suggestion says so instead of quietly overwriting their work.
  • Everyone on the ride sees everyone else's suggestions, so two people do not propose the same detour.

27 August 2026 — settings, your picture, and how things read

  • Settings is reorganized into two topics — Appearance, and how things read — instead of four boxes that never quite lined up.
  • You can switch the whole site to kilometers. Distances, your records, the roadbook, the ride list and the builder all follow it. Twistiness comes along too: the same road reads as a smaller number per kilometer than per mile.
  • You can turn animation off here without changing your whole phone or computer — or leave it following your device, which is what it has always done.
  • Your profile takes a picture now. Pick a photo of any shape, position and zoom the circle, and we store a square copy. We strip the location your camera put in it.
  • Typing an address offers suggestions, and picking one fills the street, town, state and postal code at once.
  • The profile saves as you go. Your username and your address still need the button — a username gets claimed and held the moment it is saved, so that one waits until you say so.
  • A phone number and your social handles, each with its own switch for who can see them.
  • In a club? Clubs are not built yet, but there is a note on your profile asking you to get in touch so we can find you when they are.
  • A fix worth knowing about: if your dates ever switched to American order on their own, that was us. Saving a setting could overwrite the date format your browser was giving you. It no longer can.

27 August 2026 — a louder dashboard, and deleting without losing your place

  • Deleting a ride from the dashboard no longer jumps you back to the top of the page. The card is replaced where it stood, with an Undo right there if you did not mean it — so you can clear out several rides in a row without scrolling back down each time.
  • The figures at the top of the dashboard — rides, days, legs, waypoints — are bigger. They were the smallest numbers on a page whose job is to show you numbers.
  • Each of your records now shows its figure in that record's own color, matching the icon beside it.
  • Those colors were checked against all six themes while we were in there, and several of them were genuinely hard to read on the dark ones. Every record figure has been retuned so it is legible in whichever theme you use.

27 August 2026 — dragging a stop where you want it

  • Dragging a stop to a new position works again. It had been landing a few rows farther down than you dropped it, or springing back to where it started the next time the list redrew. Both are fixed: a stop goes exactly where you put it.
  • If a drag sprang back, the change was never saved either — so a reorder you thought you had made was simply not there when you came back to the ride. Worth re-checking the order on any ride you have been rearranging this week.
  • Dragging a stop from one day into another now drops it where you aimed, instead of at the bottom of that day.

26 August 2026 — the ride panel, in three tabs

  • The panel is three tabs now: Routes, Groups and Riders. Groups and riders arrived last week and pushed the day you were actually editing off the bottom of the panel. Only one of the three takes up room at a time, so the day list gets the space back.
  • The ride's description and who can see it sit above the tabs, because they are about the whole ride rather than any one tab.
  • Riders is new. It shows who is coming, what they said, which bike they are bringing, and the smallest tank on the ride—without leaving the map. You can put a rider on a group from there, or take somebody off the ride.
  • Inviting someone, answering for yourself, and the vote are still on the riders page, and there is a link straight to it. Those are things any rider does, and the panel is only for whoever is planning it.
  • A group you have just added cannot have riders put on it until the ride saves, which takes a second or two. The tab says so rather than leaving the group mysteriously missing.
  • In the corner menu, What's new is now above Something wrong? instead of below it.
  • The build stamp under it is finally right. Every deployed build has been calling itself dev since the stamp was added, whatever it actually was—so if you have quoted one in a bug report, it told us nothing. It now says the build you are really on.

26 August 2026 — groups that split up

  • Riders leaving from different places. Add a group for each starting point in the ride panel, then say which days each group rides. The days you leave on Everyone are the ones you all ride together.
  • Everyone gets their own copy of the ride. Your roadbook, your Google Maps links and your downloads start where you start and skip somebody else's morning — and the map shows the whole shape with your own path picked out.
  • Routeloop can find the meeting point for you. Press Find a meet beside a group and it suggests places on the shared route they could join without going backwards or a long way round — preferring fuel stops, since that is where everyone regathers anyway.
  • Pick whose clock the day is built around, and whether you are pinning their departure, the meet, or getting there. If you pick a group that is closer than the others, it says so.
  • Fuel stops are planned around the smallest tank coming, and it tells you whose it is. Say which bike you are bringing on the riders page.

26 August 2026 — later

  • Riders on a ride. Open a ride and press Riders and the vote. Add the friends you are actually going with, and they get the ride on their own dashboard under Riding with others — whatever its visibility is set to.
  • Everyone on a ride says whether they are in: Going, Maybe, or can't make it. You answer for yourself, and only for yourself.
  • Vote on alternatives. If a day has two or more ways of doing it, everyone on the ride picks one and the count sits beside each. Press your own pick again to take it back.
  • The count does nothing on its own — you promote an alternative yourself, as before. Set a deadline and the one with the most votes is elected then instead. A tie leaves your current pick alone.
  • You can only add a friend to a ride. To share a route with somebody you have not added, set it to Unlisted and send the link.

26 August 2026

  • Friends. Find someone on the Riders page and ask. Once you are friends, either of you can set a ride to Friends and the other can open it — nobody else can, link or no link.
  • A fourth setting on every ride, then. Private is still only you, Unlisted is still anyone holding the link, Public is still listed in Explore, and Friends is the one that actually checks who is asking.
  • You can take a copy of a friend's ride the same way you can take a copy of a public one. Unlisted rides stay un-copyable: handing someone a link is not handing them a copy.
  • Blocking, if you need it. A blocked rider disappears from your Riders page and you from theirs, and they are not told.
  • Riders on a ride — invites, who is in, who is out — is next. There is a preview of it in the ride panel.

23 August 2026

  • Every point you drop now starts as a point of interest. Drop pins wherever you are thinking of going, in whatever order, then promote the ones the route actually has to run through — "Make this a stop" in the row's ⋮ menu. The road is drawn once two of them are stops. The first point of each day is promoted for you.
  • Changed your mind? "Make this a POI" puts it back, and the point keeps its notes, categories and reservations.
  • Dragging a row reorders it, whichever kind it is. Dragging a point of interest no longer moves its pin.
  • This page, and a version number in the footer, so you can tell which build you found something in.
  • The red button on the map is now a bug, and the grey one beside it opens this page.

22 August 2026

  • Saved places. Keep a library of the places you use — home, the good fuel stop, the meet point everyone knows — and drop one into any ride from the day's search box. File them into groups if you want to.
  • A place you save carries its phone number and address into every ride you use it in, but never a confirmation number — that belongs to one trip.
  • Buttons look like highway signs. The green field and white keyline are the house style now rather than something a few buttons opted into.
  • Fixed: you could not click into the search box at the bottom of a day. It could be tabbed to but not clicked.
  • Each day has its own button for adding a point. Press it, then click the map, and the point lands on that day.
  • The button that adds a day looked broken. It always worked — it just had no styling at all.

21 August 2026

  • Stop details. A stop can hold a confirmation number, check-in and check-out, a phone number, an address, links and notes. Only you can see them. They never go out with a shared link and they are stripped from every export except your own backup file.
  • Your rides show a map of themselves on the list, instead of all looking alike.
  • The alpha notice picked up its club marks and a bit of paint.

19 August 2026

  • Leaving the map is an "Exit map" menu item now, rather than an X you had to find.
  • Fixed: the stop-duration setting rendered wider than it declared, and the legend refused to wrap at any window size.

16 August 2026

  • Alternate days. Two or more versions of the same stretch, grouped, with one of them live. The others are drawn as dashed ghosts and stay out of your mileage, your exports and your roadbook until you make one of them the active one.
  • Tell us something. Report a bug or ask for a feature from inside the app, with a screenshot if it helps. A bug is private between you and the owner unless it gets published; the idea board is where feature wants go, and you can back someone else's instead of filing your own.
  • You get an email when something you reported changes state.
  • Every day has its own search row now, instead of one search box that had to be told which day it meant.
  • A day menu, and a select mode for acting on several days or points at once.
  • The builder's icons were redrawn by an actual human.

15 August 2026

  • The builder is a drawer down the left side showing every day at once, instead of a panel that floated over the map showing one.
  • The Save button is gone, and that is on purpose. Your work saves itself when you stop typing. If the browser dies mid-edit, the draft is waiting when you come back.
  • Drag a row to reorder it, or use the menu on the row if you would rather not drag.
  • The ride timeline moved to the bottom edge of the map, where it stops covering the road.
  • Terrain is the default map now, and there is a switcher for the others.
  • Choose how stop times are written — 1h 30m or 1:30 — in Settings.
  • The ride's name is the headline of the drawer, so you can tell two half-finished rides apart.
  • An imported file opens in the builder and can be edited, rather than being a route you could only look at.
  • Delete Me. Ask for your account to be destroyed and it goes on a 30-day hold, with a Save Me button that calls it off. Nothing skips the hold, including us.
  • Your rides live at their own address now, and old rollchart.app links land in the right place.

14 August 2026

  • Download Me. Everything the app holds about you, packaged and handed over, in one file.

13 August 2026

  • The typeface is served by us rather than borrowed from Google, so pages stop flashing an unstyled headline on the way in.

11 August 2026

  • The app is called Routeloop. It was TankBag, and briefly RouteLoop before that. Files you already downloaded still import. Anything named tankbag_… is read exactly as before, day order and dates intact — that is deliberate and it is not going away.

9 August 2026

  • A proper navigation bar on a desktop screen, with About and your account as their own menus.

8 August 2026

  • The signed-in home page is your numbers — miles, days, rides, and what you have been planning — rather than a list you already had.
  • Invitations, and a short survey for the riders who take one.
  • Downloads are named so they can be re-imported. The long filename carries the ride, the day, the date and the order, because GPX and KML cannot hold a schedule inside themselves. Drop the files back in and the ride comes back with its days in the right order.
  • Drop a file anywhere on the import page rather than hunting for a file picker.

7 August 2026

  • Mail from the app now comes from the app's own domain and looks like something a person sent — the sign-in link, a waitlist confirmation, and an approval notice.

5 August 2026

  • Undo and redo in the builder, and a recovered draft if the browser falls over.
  • Drag the route line onto the road you actually meant. The point you drop shapes that one leg and nothing else re-routes.

4 August 2026

3 August 2026

  • Fixed: a file holding several tracks kept only the longest one. It keeps all of them now.

2 August 2026

  • A printable roadbook for the tank bag: stop by stop, leg and running miles, miles since fuel, and an estimated clock.
  • Twisty. Every route gets a rough measure of how much the road actually bends, from Straight to Very twisty. Hover it for the number behind the word.
  • A point of interest can carry how long you expect to be there, and sits in the list where it falls along the road.
  • The questions page collapses into an accordion, and each answer has a link you can send someone.

  • Find a ride — a gallery of the rides other people have made public.

  • Pick a username, and a starting point that shows on a ride you make public.

Before all that, July 2026

  • The app was rebuilt from scratch in its second week, sign-in was tried three different ways before landing on Google and an emailed link, and it was called something else twice. None of that is worth a line each — but it is why the earliest rides in your account may look like they were made by a different app. They were.

Found something wrong? The red button on the map is the fastest way to tell us, and it brings the details of what you were doing with it.
