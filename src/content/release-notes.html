What’s new

Routeloop is in a closed alpha and changes most days. The newest build is at the top, and the version in the page footer tells you which one you are on.

Every entry carries a build stamp and a commit. The ones from before the version scheme existed were read back out of the commit log — the stamp is the last commit of that day — so you can see how the app got to you.

14 September 2026 — a ride you can carry with no signal, a lighter download, a brighter dark mode, and your own choice of map tiles 2026-09-14-1952PT 211d645

  • New

    Every ride has an On the road page for your phone: the Google Maps legs one tap at a time, the ride’s files handed to your nav app, the roadbook as a list, and a copy you can keep for when there is no signal.

    • Find it beside Roadbook on the ride page. Tap a leg and Maps starts from where you are; your place is remembered on that phone, and Next leg is one big button.
    • GPX, KML and the Routeloop file go to your nav app through the share sheet where the phone allows it, or land in Downloads. On an iPhone pick Save to Files and open the file from there—most nav apps do not show up in the sheet directly.
    • Keep on this phone stores the page, the roadbook and the files, so they open with no connection. Nothing is kept until you press it, and Remove takes it back.
    • Routeloop can be added to your Home Screen, and a page opened with no signal lists the rides you kept.
  • Fixed

    The Google Maps hand-off links start from where you are, so Maps offers Start rather than Preview.

    • The old navigate page said they did and they did not. It lives at the new address now and the old one forwards.
  • New

    The map can be light or dark on its own, under Preferences → Appearance → Map tiles.

    • It matches the page unless you say otherwise, so a dark page gets dark tiles and a light page light ones.
    • Pick Light or Dark to keep the tiles one way whatever the page is. The map picks it up the next time it opens.
  • Changed

    Your stats on the dashboard are three bars each: you, the average rider, and the top rider, so you can see at a glance where you stand.

    • Green is you, blue is the average rider, orange is the top rider, and every bar is drawn against the top rider’s figure.
    • The tiles sit under a Your stats heading, each reads “13 rides” on one line, and Plan a ride moved up beside your miles planned.
  • Fixed

    Dark mode is easier to read: the text and the gray captions are lighter, and the route grips in the builder carry more of their route’s color.

    • The Changed and Removed badges on this page take black lettering in every scheme; they had gone gray on their fields under a dark page.
    • The Take the tour sign on the builder and the viewer had kept a green border from before it turned yellow.
  • Changed

    The app’s scripts arrive at about a quarter of their old size, so the builder and the viewer open faster on a slow connection.

    • Nothing on screen changes; the same code is sent with its notes and spacing taken out.

11 September 2026 — the full guided tour 2026-09-11-2203PT f3a786f

    The tour is now the whole app, in five parts.

    • It plans one ride from start to finish while you watch — Oakland to Santa Cruz by Alice’s and Pescadero — and the only buttons are Next, Back and Skip.

It ends on the page you would share. About thirty cards, any part skippable.
  • New

    Three riders exist for it.

    • Sam, Priya and Diego are on every tour ride with real bikes and real ranges, so the roster, the fuel ring and the split have somebody to be about.
    • They are nowhere else: not on the riders page, not friendable, never mailed.
  • New

    The ride it builds is real, and it goes in the bin when you finish.

    • It was the tour’s, not yours.
    • Nothing is added to your paddock and nothing is routed live — the demo was planned once and is replayed, so it is the same every time and costs nothing to run.
  • New

    It follows you across pages.

    • The roster on the ride’s own page, the paddock on your profile, the riders page, and the shared view of the ride are each visited when the story gets there.
  • New

    A small Take the tour sign sits beside your name in the header, on the builder too.

    • If you have seen enough of it, Show me around in Preferences has a checkbox that takes it away; Take the tour under the menu stays either way.
  • Fixed

    A route’s Starts and Ends fields opened blank on a dated ride until you touched something; the times were there the whole time.

10 September 2026 — a builder tour, tooltips, and the unread dot 2026-09-10-2045PT d9d16d9

  • New

  • New

    • Every button, field and grip in the builder, the download row and the buttons on a ride page, the tabs on your dashboard, the footer: a headline and a sentence on why you would touch it, faster than the old tooltip.
    • The reverse arrow says the road back is not always the road out.
    • The bed-time field says what the band across your route list means.
  • New

    Both are on to start with, and neither turns itself off.

    • Preferences has a new Show me around section at the top with the tour button and the switch for the tips.
    • Say no to the tips and you get the short labels back exactly as they were, on every device you sign in on.
  • Fixed

    A ride you moved to the bin no longer sits on your dashboard.

    • If somebody else put you on a ride and then deleted it, the card stayed under “riding with” and opening it answered not found.
    • It was the one list in the app that did not check the bin, so the only thing you could do about it was ignore it. That card is gone.
  • Fixed

    The unread dot now shows on the map.

    • The red count on your account chip is the way to your notifications, and on the builder and the ride viewer that chip lives inside the menu — so nothing on screen told you anything had happened and you had to open the menu on the off chance.

    • It says something is in there rather than how many.
  • Fixed

    A wrong link now lands on a page instead of a line of text.

    • A mistyped address, or a shared ride that has since been deleted or made private, used to answer with the words Not found on a blank page and no way onward.
    • It is a proper page now, and it keeps the menu so you can get back to what you were doing.
  • Fixed

    The blue on a route where riders join or leave is readable again.

    • It was set in a blue borrowed from another app’s logo, which was too pale to read as text and could not be darkened by the high-contrast theme — the one setting that exists to fix exactly that.

9 September 2026 — staging on live data, color-coded notifications, and release notes as notifications 2026-09-09-2026PT 0e67541

  • Changed

    Staging is not a sandbox any more.

    • It used to hold its own throwaway rides, which meant nothing was ever tried against real ones until it was already live.
    • It now reads and writes the same rides, the same riders and the same uploads as the real site.
  • Changed

    So anything you delete on staging is really deleted.

    • Bin a ride there and it is a rider’s ride; empty the bin and it is gone for good.
    • The amber banner says so on every page now — it used to promise the opposite, that everything there gets wiped, and that is no longer true.
  • Changed

    Staging will never email anybody.

    • Testing a notification there cannot reach a real rider.
    • The side effect is that the sign-in link does not arrive on staging either, so use Sign in with Google to get in.
  • Changed

    Staging is a button now, like the real site.

    • It no longer deploys itself every time something merges, because that would put new code in front of real data with nobody deciding to.
  • New

    Notifications are color-coded by what they are.

    • A comment is gray, a suggestion yellow, a settled vote green, someone joining a ride blue, a friend request brown, a report you filed red, and a release the guide-sign green.
    • They were all the same blue before, so the little picture was doing all the work and the color none of it.
  • New

    Storage still speaks twice.

    • Nearly out of room is amber, and something about to be destroyed is red — the one mark that wears two colors, because those are advice and a verdict and should not look the same.
  • Changed

    A notification you can open now says so.

    • Rows that lead somewhere carry a small green Read more sign at the end of the line.
    • Rows with nowhere to go do not, so you can tell at a glance which is which.
  • Fixed

    The unread count is round.

    • It was half a pixel taller than it was wide, the number inside it sat high, and in the menu the dot hung low and too close to the word. All measured and all fixed.
  • New

    Release notes now arrive as a notification.

    • When a build ships, it turns up in your notifications alongside everything else, newest first — so you find out what changed without going to look for it.
  • New

    Every release is in there, not just the new ones.

    • The whole history back to July is in your notifications, dated when it actually shipped, already marked read so nothing is shouting at you.
    • Each one links to its own entry on this page, which still has the full text.
  • New

    The red count on your name is the nudge.

    • It sits on your name in the header, and again on Notifications in that menu so you know where you are going.
    • On the route builder and the map the header collapses to the ☰ menu, so the count is inside there rather than on screen — those two had no way to reach the release notes at all since the feedback button went.
  • New

    No email unless you ask for one.

    • Routeloop ships several times a day, so release notes are the one notification that is off on email and off in the browser to start with.
    • If you want them in your inbox, Preferences → Notifications → Your account has the switch.

6 September 2026 — days become routes, and riders per route 2026-09-06-2323PT 9de6ab7

  • Changed

    What used to be called a day is now called a route.

    • Nothing about your rides has changed — the same list, the same order, the same colors. Only the word.
    • Once you plan for more than one group, the first few routes are often fractions of one afternoon rather than days at all: three groups setting off from three towns and meeting up before lunch.
    • Calling those “days” described none of them.
  • New

    “Day” still means a day.

    • Dates, start times and the clock on your routes are unchanged — a Saturday is still a Saturday.
    • Release notes below this one still say “day” because that is what they said at the time, and they are a record rather than instructions.
  • New

    A route can be ridden by several groups, not just one.

    • The group control on a route is a list of tickboxes now.
    • Once two groups meet up, the road they share says both their names instead of claiming everybody rides it — which was wrong whenever a third group was still on its way. Ticking Everyone ticks the lot.
    • Your own group is remembered on the road you rode to get there, so when you peel off for home it can offer to split you back off the same way.
  • New

    You can say who rides which route.

    • Each route in the Routes panel now shows how many riders are on it, and tapping that opens a list to tick.
    • Somebody who joins you for the middle of a ride and peels off again is two answers — everyone stays on from where you put them until you say otherwise — and the routes where the group changes read “Dylan joins here” and “Dylan leaves here” instead of a count.
  • Changed

    Your roadbook and your files are yours alone now.

    • Download the roadbook, the Google Maps hand-off or any of the four file formats and you get the routes you ride — not the whole group’s.
    • A friend who joined for one afternoon gets that afternoon.
    • This used to give you your group’s whole run, which was the same answer only when everybody in a group rode exactly the same stretches.
  • Changed

    Picking a meeting point now splits the shared stretch off as its own route.

    • Everybody’s approach stays their own, and the road you ride together from the meeting point becomes one route marked for everyone.
    • Do it twice and you get a staged meet-up — two groups join at the first point and ride on together, and the third catches everyone at the second.
  • Changed

    Departure times now move the whole approach.

    • If a group has a route or two before the one that reaches the meeting point, those shift with it instead of being left behind.
  • New

    You can say how far a detour is worth it.

    • There is a miles box beside Find meeting points now.
    • Widen it and you will be offered somewhere earlier on the road; tighten it and the suggestion moves closer to where everybody was already going.
  • New

    Groups show where they start.

    • Each group in the Groups panel now says which town it sets off from, under its name.
  • New

    Shaping points snap to the road.

    • Drag the line onto a different highway and the handle now settles on the road itself rather than wherever your pointer landed.
  • New

    Meeting-point suggestions sit on top of everything else on the map.

    • A numbered suggestion could end up behind a gas-station pin, where you could neither see it nor hover it.
  • Changed

    Re-check your saved ride files.

    • The file format moved on to carry the new wording.
    • Anything you exported before today still imports exactly as it did — but if you keep backups, this is a good day to drop one back on the import page and confirm your routes come back the way you left them.

3 September 2026 — better meeting points, and readable save errors 2026-09-03-1851PT 0b260c5

  • Changed

    Find meeting points now answers the question you actually have.

    • Plan your main group’s route to the destination — say Oakland to Ensenada — then press Find meeting points in the Groups panel.
    • It suggests where the other groups should join that road, and tells you what each one goes out of its way by name.
    • Where a group’s route already passes through the spot, it says so rather than showing a zero.
  • Changed

    There is now one button for the whole ride instead of one per group, and the first group you add is the main group.

    • It used to refuse to say anything until a day was marked as ridden by everyone — which is the thing you were trying to work out.
  • Fixed

    Deleting a stop could leave a day that would never save again.

    • If you removed the last stop from a day using the row menu, everything left on it was a point you ride past rather than stop at — and the ride refused to save from then on, with no way to put it right except tagging one by hand.
    • The first remaining point now becomes the stop, the way it already did everywhere else.
  • Changed

    Every ride now starts with one group already there, which is you.

    • Rename it if you like; you cannot delete it, because a ride you planned has at least one rider on it.
    • Groups only start appearing elsewhere — on the roster, and on the roadbook’s whose-copy switch — once there are two of them.
  • Changed

    The Groups tab now shows the main group on its own at the top, marked Main.

    • That is the group whose route everyone else joins when you look for a meeting point.
    • To change it, drag another group up over it — the one on top is the main one.
    • You can also focus a group’s grip and use the arrow keys.
  • Changed

    Suggested meeting points now appear on the map.

    • One numbered dot each, matching the list, with the road each joining group would ride to reach it drawn alongside.
    • Hover a dot or a row and the other lights up; press either to take it.
  • New

    Tell a ride when you like to stop for the night.

    • Set a time on the ride — say 4pm — and the day list breaks where you reach that hour, with a band saying how far in you are and how much riding would still be left after it.
    • It takes the road you planned and the stops you added into account, and offers to search that stretch for somewhere to stay; picking one still offers to end the day there. The map marks the spot too. Leave it blank and nothing changes.
  • Fixed

    Dragging a route onto a different road stopped working.

    • It broke earlier the same day, when the search-result dots landed — two pieces of the map code ended up sharing a name and the newer one silently replaced the older.
    • Dragging a leg to reshape it works again.
  • Fixed

    Searching a long day for gas missed most of it.

    • Looking for fuel along the day ran a fixed six searches spread across the whole route, and on anything over about 190 miles those searches stopped overlapping — so a 593-mile day had 37-mile stretches nobody ever looked at, and a run through Los Angeles could come back with nothing.
    • It now runs as many searches as the day needs, which also means fewer of them on a short day.
    • Past about 745 miles it still cannot cover everything, and now says so rather than looking like an empty road.
  • New

    A meeting point has to be one everybody can actually get to on the fuel they have.

    • Suggestions are now checked against the shortest tank on the ride, measured from each group’s last fill — along the road for the main group, and along the real routed road for anyone joining.
    • If nothing is reachable without stopping for fuel first, it says so rather than quietly suggesting a forecourt you would run dry short of.
  • New

    Meeting points are gas stations.

    • Everyone turns up needing fuel and a forecourt is somewhere you can actually wait, so the suggestions are real stations on the main group’s road, named and with an address.
    • The point added to everyone’s day is the station itself rather than a spot on the highway beside it, and it counts as a refuel on the fuel gauge.
    • If there is no station on the stretch everyone can reach, it offers the best plain spots instead and says that is what it did.
  • Changed

    Meeting points are now the earliest everyone can reach, not the cheapest.

    • The old suggestions were whichever cost the joining group the fewest extra miles, which is almost always somewhere near the end of the ride — so two groups rode most of the way separately to a ride they were doing together.

  • New

    • The main group keeps the time you gave them, and every other group is told when to leave so they arrive at the meeting point at the same moment.

    • If the main group’s day has no date yet, it says so instead of quietly doing nothing.
  • Removed

    • It is always the main group’s departure, which is the one time anybody actually knows; the meet and the arrival are worked out from it.
  • Fixed

    • Every bend in the road ahead was counted as a detour the joining group had chosen, which on a normal winding route added tens of miles nobody was riding.
  • Fixed

    The second day of a ride could not be saved.

    • A new day starts where the last one ended, and that carried-over point was not being counted as a stop — so the ride failed to save, every time, until you gave one of its points a category by hand.

  • Fixed

  • Changed

    Errors name the day rather than a number.

    • “Day 2 needs at least one stop”, not “days.1”.
