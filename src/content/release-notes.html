What's new

Routeloop is in a closed alpha and changes most days. The newest build is at the top, and the version in the page footer tells you which one you are on.

Everything below is headed by day rather than by build. It predates the version stamp — and the alpha — and was read back out of the commit log, so you can see how the app got to you.

26 August 2026

  • Friends. Find someone on the Riders page and ask. Once you are friends, either of you can set a ride to Friends and the other can open it — nobody else can, link or no link.
  • A fourth setting on every ride, then. Private is still only you, Unlisted is still anyone holding the link, Public is still listed in Explore, and Friends is the one that actually checks who is asking.
  • You can take a copy of a friend's ride the same way you can take a copy of a public one. Unlisted rides stay un-copyable: handing someone a link is not handing them a copy.
  • Blocking, if you need it. A blocked rider disappears from your Riders page and you from theirs, and they are not told.
  • Riders on a ride — invites, who is in, who is out — is next. There is a preview of it in the ride panel.

23 August 2026

  • Every point you drop now starts as a point of interest. Drop pins wherever you are thinking of going, in whatever order, then promote the ones the route actually has to run through — "Make this a stop" in the row's ⋮ menu. The road is drawn once two of them are stops. The first point of each day is promoted for you.
  • Changed your mind? "Make this a POI" puts it back, and the point keeps its notes, categories and reservations.
  • Dragging a row reorders it, whichever kind it is. Dragging a point of interest no longer moves its pin.
  • This page, and a version number in the footer, so you can tell which build you found something in.
  • The red button on the map is now a bug, and the grey one beside it opens this page.

22 August 2026

  • Saved places. Keep a library of the places you use — home, the good fuel stop, the meet point everyone knows — and drop one into any ride from the day's search box. File them into groups if you want to.
  • A place you save carries its phone number and address into every ride you use it in, but never a confirmation number — that belongs to one trip.
  • Buttons look like highway signs. The green field and white keyline are the house style now rather than something a few buttons opted into.
  • Fixed: you could not click into the search box at the bottom of a day. It could be tabbed to but not clicked.
  • Each day has its own button for adding a point. Press it, then click the map, and the point lands on that day.
  • The button that adds a day looked broken. It always worked — it just had no styling at all.

21 August 2026

  • Stop details. A stop can hold a confirmation number, check-in and check-out, a phone number, an address, links and notes. Only you can see them. They never go out with a shared link and they are stripped from every export except your own backup file.
  • Your rides show a map of themselves on the list, instead of all looking alike.
  • The alpha notice picked up its club marks and a bit of paint.

19 August 2026

  • Leaving the map is an "Exit map" menu item now, rather than an X you had to find.
  • Fixed: the stop-duration setting rendered wider than it declared, and the legend refused to wrap at any window size.

16 August 2026

  • Alternate days. Two or more versions of the same stretch, grouped, with one of them live. The others are drawn as dashed ghosts and stay out of your mileage, your exports and your roadbook until you make one of them the active one.
  • Tell us something. Report a bug or ask for a feature from inside the app, with a screenshot if it helps. A bug is private between you and the owner unless it gets published; the idea board is where feature wants go, and you can back someone else's instead of filing your own.
  • You get an email when something you reported changes state.
  • Every day has its own search row now, instead of one search box that had to be told which day it meant.
  • A day menu, and a select mode for acting on several days or points at once.
  • The builder's icons were redrawn by an actual human.

15 August 2026

  • The builder is a drawer down the left side showing every day at once, instead of a panel that floated over the map showing one.
  • The Save button is gone, and that is on purpose. Your work saves itself when you stop typing. If the browser dies mid-edit, the draft is waiting when you come back.
  • Drag a row to reorder it, or use the menu on the row if you would rather not drag.
  • The ride timeline moved to the bottom edge of the map, where it stops covering the road.
  • Terrain is the default map now, and there is a switcher for the others.
  • Choose how stop times are written — 1h 30m or 1:30 — in Settings.
  • The ride's name is the headline of the drawer, so you can tell two half-finished rides apart.
  • An imported file opens in the builder and can be edited, rather than being a route you could only look at.
  • Delete Me. Ask for your account to be destroyed and it goes on a 30-day hold, with a Save Me button that calls it off. Nothing skips the hold, including us.
  • Your rides live at their own address now, and old rollchart.app links land in the right place.

14 August 2026

  • Download Me. Everything the app holds about you, packaged and handed over, in one file.

13 August 2026

  • The typeface is served by us rather than borrowed from Google, so pages stop flashing an unstyled headline on the way in.

11 August 2026

  • The app is called Routeloop. It was TankBag, and briefly RouteLoop before that. Files you already downloaded still import. Anything named tankbag_… is read exactly as before, day order and dates intact — that is deliberate and it is not going away.

9 August 2026

  • A proper navigation bar on a desktop screen, with About and your account as their own menus.

8 August 2026

  • The signed-in home page is your numbers — miles, days, rides, and what you have been planning — rather than a list you already had.
  • Invitations, and a short survey for the riders who take one.
  • Downloads are named so they can be re-imported. The long filename carries the ride, the day, the date and the order, because GPX and KML cannot hold a schedule inside themselves. Drop the files back in and the ride comes back with its days in the right order.
  • Drop a file anywhere on the import page rather than hunting for a file picker.

7 August 2026

  • Mail from the app now comes from the app's own domain and looks like something a person sent — the sign-in link, a waitlist confirmation, and an approval notice.

5 August 2026

  • Undo and redo in the builder, and a recovered draft if the browser falls over.
  • Drag the route line onto the road you actually meant. The point you drop shapes that one leg and nothing else re-routes.

4 August 2026

  • Hand a day off to Google Maps as an ordered series of links.
  • Expand weaves extra shaping points into those links so Maps has too little room to pick its own roads — and it tells you the longest stretch Maps still chooses for itself rather than hiding it.

3 August 2026

  • Fixed: a file holding several tracks kept only the longest one. It keeps all of them now.

2 August 2026

  • Import and export. KML, KMZ, GPX, GeoJSON and CSV in and out, plus a lossless Routeloop backup file that re-imports as the same ride. Hand it several files at once and they become the days of one ride.
  • A printable roadbook for the tank bag: stop by stop, leg and running miles, miles since fuel, and an estimated clock.
  • Twisty. Every route gets a rough measure of how much the road actually bends, from Straight to Very twisty. Hover it for the number behind the word.
  • A point of interest can carry how long you expect to be there, and sits in the list where it falls along the road.
  • The questions page collapses into an accordion, and each answer has a link you can send someone.

1 August 2026

  • A timeline slider across the whole ride. Drag it and the day it lands on comes forward while the rest dim, on both the builder and the shared view.
  • Find a ride — a gallery of the rides other people have made public.
  • Clone a public ride into your own account and change it however you like.
  • Pick a username, and a starting point that shows on a ride you make public.
  • Reverse a day, and throw away unsaved changes without reloading.
  • Pause the video on the sign-in page.

31 July 2026

  • Give a day a start and end time, and get an honest estimate for a leg the router could not route rather than a zero.

30 July 2026

  • The map engine moved to Google Maps, and a ride became several days drawn together on one map instead of a single route.

Before all that, July 2026

  • The app was rebuilt from scratch in its second week, sign-in was tried three different ways before landing on Google and an emailed link, and it was called something else twice. None of that is worth a line each — but it is why the earliest rides in your account may look like they were made by a different app. They were.

Found something wrong? The red button on the map is the fastest way to tell us, and it brings the details of what you were doing with it.
