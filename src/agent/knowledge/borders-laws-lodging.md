# Borders, laws, and lodging

How to use this: general shape only. Border rules, state laws, park reservations, and fees change often; never state one as current fact without the official source, and always give the year with any dated fact below.

## Mexico

Verify everything here with the official source before a trip.

- Insurance: a U.S. auto or motorcycle policy generally does not cover Mexico. Riders need Mexican liability insurance from a Mexico-licensed insurer, bought by the trip or the year. Without it, an accident can mean detention.
- Documents: a valid passport, license, and the vehicle's registration (plus written permission if the vehicle is not in the rider's name).
- Tourist permit (FMM): needed for stays beyond the border area or beyond a short visit; there is a fee for longer stays. Sources disagree on whether the free short stay is 6 or 7 days by land, and the fee changes yearly.
- Temporary Import Permit (TIP): the Baja California peninsula and the Sonora "free zone" near the border do not require one. Taking a U.S.-plated vehicle, motorcycles included, deeper into mainland Mexico requires a TIP from Banjercito, with a refundable deposit. The TIP must be canceled at a Banjercito office on the way out or the deposit is lost and the vehicle can be flagged.
- Baja: carry cash for fuel (many remote stations take cash only), expect military and agricultural checkpoints on Highway 1, and do not ride at night (livestock, no shoulders, potholes).
- Firearms are a serious crime to bring into Mexico. Prescriptions stay in original labeled bottles.
- U.S. State Department travel advisories differ by Mexican state; check them for each state on the route at trip time.
- Official sources: <https://www.banjercito.com.mx>, <https://www.inm.gob.mx>, <https://travel.state.gov>

## Canada

- Insurance: many U.S. policies extend to Canada, but not all. Riders should confirm with their insurer and carry the Canadian non-resident insurance card or a letter from the insurer. It usually takes a phone call.
- ID: a passport is accepted everywhere; some states issue enhanced driver's licenses valid for land crossings. Borrowed vehicle: registration plus written permission.
- Cannabis: bringing any cannabis product across the border, including CBD and edibles, is a serious offense even if it is legal in the rider's state. None in the saddlebags.
- Weapons: declare everything. Handguns and many other weapons are refused; pepper spray made for use on people is prohibited, while bear spray labeled for animals is generally allowed. Check before going.
- Small crossings can have limited hours and close in the evening; busy crossings can mean long waits on weekends. Leave a cushion.
- Speed limits are in km/h (100–110 km/h is about 62–68 mph on most highways). Helmets are required for motorcyclists across Canada; verify per province.
- Official sources: <https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca>, <https://bwt.cbp.gov> (U.S. border wait times), <https://travel.state.gov>

## U.S. state differences

The law that applies is the state the rider is in, not the state that issued their license.

- Helmets (IIHS table, as of 2026): 17 states plus D.C. require helmets for all riders—Alabama, California, Georgia, Louisiana, Maryland, Massachusetts, Mississippi, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Oregon, Tennessee, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, and West Virginia. Illinois, Iowa, and New Hampshire have no helmet law. Most others require helmets for younger riders only. Other counts circulating online are older.
- Lane splitting and filtering (as of 2026; verify): California is the only state that allows lane splitting in moving traffic. Utah, Arizona, Montana, Colorado, and Minnesota allow filtering under narrow conditions (typically low speeds, stopped or slow traffic, and lower-speed roads). Oregon's and Hawaii's status is reported inconsistently. Elsewhere, assume it is illegal. Groups should not split or filter as a pack.
- Eye protection: several states require a windshield or eye protection; there is no single verified list. A face shield or glasses everywhere is the safe default.
- Headlight on at all times is required in many states and is a sensible default everywhere.
- Radar detectors: generally legal for private vehicles except in Virginia and D.C.
- Speed limits change at state lines, near towns, and in work zones. Rural interstate limits range from 60 to 85 mph by state.
- Laws on cannabis and carrying weapons differ sharply from state to state; riders should read up on each state they pass through and agree as a group on what anyone is carrying.

## National parks (dated facts)

Reservation systems change every year. Encode these as "as of 2026" and send the rider to the park's page.

- Rocky Mountain (as of 2026): timed entry from May 22 to October 12, 2026, required only between 9 am and 2 pm; entering before 9 or after 2 needs no reservation. One reservation covers two motorcycles traveling together. Trail Ridge Road falls under the same rule. Official page: <https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/timed-entry-permit-system.htm>
- Glacier (as of 2026): no vehicle reservation required in 2026; instead, a ticketed Logan Pass shuttle and a 3-hour parking limit at Logan Pass. The system has changed nearly every year since 2021. Going-to-the-Sun Road bars vehicles over 21 feet long or 8 feet wide on its central section—motorcycles are fine, but a trailer or support van may not be. Official page: <https://www.nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/vehicle-reservations.htm>
- Yosemite (as of 2026): no timed reservation in 2026; expect heavy weekend and holiday traffic.
- Arches (as of 2026): no timed entry in 2026, per a park release; some specific areas and campgrounds still need their own reservations.
- All parks charge an entrance fee or accept an annual pass; motorcycles may have their own pass category.
- Reservations and campsites: <https://www.recreation.gov>

## Lodging

- Book ahead for: groups of about 8 or more (small motels have 10–30 rooms), national park gateway towns in peak season (late June to early September and fall color weekends), any town during a rally or event, holiday weekends, and one-motel towns.
- Winging it works for: weekday nights outside holidays, off-season, and larger towns with several chain hotels near the highway.
- For nights booked more than a few weeks out, prefer free cancellation up to a day or two before; weather, smoke, or a closed pass can change the route.
- In-park lodges at the big parks often release rooms about a year ahead and sell out; gateway motels fill by spring for summer.
- Motorcycle-friendly lodging: ground-floor or exterior-corridor rooms where the bikes are visible, well-lit parking near the door, covered parking in rain. Riders bring a disc lock and cover.

## Rally weeks

- During a big motorcycle rally, lodging within roughly 60–90 miles is booked or surge-priced months ahead, often with multi-night minimums and prepayment. Either book very early or route around and stay well outside the zone.
- Sturgis (South Dakota) is in early to mid-August (reported as August 7–16 for 2026; confirm with the official rally site). Other big ones: Daytona Bike Week in March, Laconia in June, Americade in Lake George in June, and Biketoberfest in Daytona in October. Dates change yearly.

## Camping

- BLM dispersed camping: commonly 14 days within a 28-day period, but limits vary by field office. U.S. Forest Service limits are set per forest. One-night stays rarely hit these limits.
- On national forests, motorized travel and roadside camping must follow the Motor Vehicle Use Map; check it before riding off pavement.
- Some BLM areas require permits or lotteries.
- Summer fire restrictions can ban campfires or even stoves on public land; check the managing office.
- Federal developed campgrounds on Recreation.gov usually open bookings on a rolling window of about six months; popular ones fill the moment they open. First-come sites fill by early afternoon on peak weekends.
- Private campsite listings can be good value, but check the access road; a steep or unpaved approach is a real problem for a loaded touring bike.
- A common camping rhythm on a motorcycle is a couple of nights camping between motel nights, with a laundry and resupply stop every few days.

## Cars and bicycles

- Cars: the same border insurance and permit rules apply; RVs and trailers hit park vehicle-size limits that motorcycles do not.
- Bicycles: no insurance or vehicle permit is needed at the border, but the same passport and cannabis rules apply. Lane-splitting laws do not apply; bicycle rules on shoulders and highways vary by state, and some freeways ban bicycles.
